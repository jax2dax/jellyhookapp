# Round 1 — Agent D (cold outsider)

Note: V2, V3 and V4 are the same below the hero. They differ only in the headline and subhead. V1 differs in the hero panel, the order of the sections, the FAQ, and its separate "does not do" list.

## V1 — "Every form lead arrives with *the visit behind it*."
**A) 5-sec:** What: partly. "The visit behind it" is abstract, and the hero chart is dense with no plain summary. Who: yes (the eyebrow line). Why care: partly. Next: yes.
**B)** Believability is high: sample data is labelled and the limits are listed openly. Hype: none, apart from "read in ten seconds". Friction: "Hook answers it" names something that is never explained. The hero chart needs the "How to read it" section to make sense. "Will it slow my site down? We have not published a speed measurement" raises a doubt and gives no number.
**C)** "Hook answers it…" (breaks if the feature is renamed). "Two ways to use it" sits over three blocks. "Mark the lead Qualified or Junk", "Settings has a tracking check" and "Setup notices the first visit" all depend on current screen names. "Does not sync to your CRM or export data yet" and "CRM sync today" will go stale. The framework and form-vendor lists will go stale too. Promises: "read in ten seconds", "nothing switches to paid on its own".
**D)** Clarity 7, Relevance 7, Believability 9, Ease 8, Desire 6, Action 7
= 1.4 + 1.4 + 1.8 + 1.2 + 0.9 + 0.7 = **7.4**

## V3 — "See which field *loses your leads*, and what the people who sent it read first."
**A)** What: partly. There are two ideas in one line, and "sent it" grammatically points at "field", not "form". Who: yes. Why: yes for the person improving forms, weak for sales. Next: yes.
**B)** Believability: "loses your leads" claims a cause that time-on-field data cannot prove. Hype: "loses your leads". Friction: the subhead is 45 words and says "send it" twice. Sales readers have to reach the last clause before they find themselves.
**C)** Same as V2. The headline also ties the page to field timing, which becomes the wrong lead if that feature changes.
**D)** Clarity 6, Relevance 7, Believability 8, Ease 9, Desire 7, Action 7
= 1.2 + 1.4 + 1.6 + 1.35 + 1.05 + 0.7 = **7.3**

## V4 — "Your form sends you a name. *Here is the rest.*"
**A)** What: yes. Who: yes. Why: yes. The gap between "a name" and "the rest" is the pain, and every lead owner feels it. Next: yes.
**B)** Believability is high. Hype: none in the hero. Friction: same as V2. The first screen never says it is one script tag, and it never mentions privacy.
**C)** Same as V2. The headline itself is future-proof: "the rest" survives any change to the feature set.

## What mattered most
1. **The headline frame.** V4 starts from what the reader already has (a name) and offers what they lack. V1/V2 describe the product's output. V3 makes a causal claim in a line that is hard to parse.
2. **A plain-sentence summary in the hero panel (V2–V4).** It makes the product clear in 5 seconds. V1's chart only makes sense after its tutorial section.
3. **Concrete IT/legal facts (V2–V4)** such as 31 KB, no cookies and the CRM workaround. They replace V1's "we have not published" non-answer.
4. **V1's undefined "Hook"** takes it out of contention.

**Strongest headline:** "Your form sends you a name. *Here is the rest.*" (V4)
**Weakest line:** "When you have a question of your own, Hook answers it across all your visits and leads." (V1). It names something the reader has never heard of, and it promises an answer to any question.
**Shared defect:** every variant puts three blocks under the heading "TWO WAYS TO USE IT".

## Changes to V4 (exact wording)
1. **Add a privacy line to the first screen.** Below "FREE DURING EARLY ACCESS · EVERY FEATURE · NO CARD", add: "One script tag. No screen, mouse or keystroke recording. Visitors stay anonymous until they send a form."
2. **Fix the label count.** Change "TWO WAYS TO USE IT" to "WHO OPENS IT", or keep it and change the H2 to "One lead for the follow-up. *Everyone* for the form. One page for the team."
3. **Remove the overpromise.** Replace "and get the answer across all your visits, with the people behind it" with "and see the answer from your own visits, with the leads it is based on."
