# P11 Grace Holloway (sceptic) - Round 2

Outcomes: V2 skim and leave | R2A interested | R2B would sign up | R2C interested

## V2
Outcome: skim and leave.
Hook: "It stays off when the browser sends Global Privacy Control or Do Not Track" and "We have not published a page-load timing yet" (honest, but buried in the setup section, far past my 20 seconds).
Push away: first screen gives me no data-handling reassurance at all. "Every form lead arrives with the visit behind it" is a benefit line, and "Read them all before you change a page" is just selling.
Scores: clarity 7, relevance 5, believability 6, ease of next step 6, desire 4, action 3.

## R2A
Outcome: interested.
Hook: "One script tag. No screen, mouse or keystroke recording. Visitors stay anonymous until they send a form." Right under the button, so I see it in the first 20 seconds.
Push away: "Your form sends you a name. Here is the rest." is cute, and "the rest" is vague. Mentioning the people who never sent the form only in the second sentence makes it feel like two products.
Scores: clarity 7, relevance 7, believability 7, ease 7, desire 6, action 5.

## R2B
Outcome: would sign up (on a test or staging site, with my developer, not on live).
Hook: same trust line, plus a hero that says plainly what I get: "what each person read before they sent your form, and which field the people who never sent it stopped on." That is concrete and I can picture it. The "Not for you if" list and "does not make you compliant" are what make me believe the rest.
Push away: "Read them all before you change a page or a form" is mild hype. And "31 KB, no page-load timing published" tells me the site-impact claim is unproven. Nothing about what the script sends or where the data is stored.
Scores: clarity 8, relevance 8, believability 7, ease 8, desire 6, action 6.

## R2C
Outcome: interested.
Hook: the same trust line and limits as the others.
Push away: the headline is long and does two jobs: "See what they read, and where the others stopped". I had to read it twice. It is also the most feature-y of the four.
Scores: clarity 6, relevance 7, believability 7, ease 7, desire 5, action 5.

## Which I would act on
R2B. It is the only one with a headline that is easy to read, a plain description of the product and the reassurance line, all on the first screen. The page and facts section are the same across the three R2s, so the hero decides it. V2 loses because the reassurance is buried below where I stop reading.

## One change that would move me a step further
Put a named data fact in the hero or the facts list: where the data is stored, what leaves the browser, and what "anonymous until they send a form" means once they do (do you then store their name and email against the visit?). Also publish a page-load timing, even a rough one. That gives my DPO and developer something to check. And one line on what happens when "early access" ends, with a rough price range, so a free trial is not hiding a bill. With those I would install it on staging today instead of just signing up.
