# Pain points for outbound (from the persona simulation, page v1)

Date: 2026-10-09. Source: `persona-results/`. These are **simulated** voices written by role-play agents from persona cards, not
real quotes. Validate the phrasing in real conversations before using it as "what customers say".

Per the brief this file covers the personas who ended **interested** or **would install today**. Only one reached interested
(P06). Because one is too few to plan outbound from, a second section lists the **near misses**: right-fit personas who named the
pain clearly and said exactly what would move them up. They are marked as near misses, not as interested.

## A. Interested

### P06: freelance CRO consultant (B2B clients, low traffic)

- **Pain in their words:** "Every new client starts with two weeks working out where the funnel leaks, and heatmaps on low-traffic
  B2B sites are noise."
- **Trigger phrases:** "form field analytics B2B", "form abandonment by field", "form field drop-off low traffic", "CRO audit tool for
  form leads", "privacy-friendly form analytics".
- **Hook line that worked:** "See where your form slows people down, and the route converters took."
- **Objections:** several client sites per account? a plain list of data collected to show a client's legal person; how it behaves at
  40 leads a month; an export or something shareable for the client.
- **Where to find them:** freelance and independent CRO consultants on LinkedIn (titles: "CRO consultant", "conversion optimisation
  consultant", "freelance CRO"); CRO newsletters and communities; people publishing CRO audits or teardowns.
- **Outbound angle that fits:** "A first-week audit input for low-traffic B2B clients: per-field timing and abandoned forms, tied to
  what each converter read. Free while in early access." Lead with field timing, not with sales follow-up. Offer to set it up on one
  client site. Do not promise multi-site until the 1-site limit is resolved.

## Patterns across all of them

- The words they use: "which field", "drop off", "stall", "what they read first", "before the first reply", "real or junk",
  "another tab", "in the CRM".
- The two things every near miss wanted: **the visit where they already work** (CRM or email) and **facts they can forward**
  (script size, data location, DPA, price).
- CRO people respond to field timing first; sales and managers respond to "same lead, same view" and "read what they read".
